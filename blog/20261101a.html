French protests

Since the middle of September (yes around the time of my birthday), across France erupted have demonstrations and protests against poor school conditions and rising fuel prices, which are part of a wider European unrest, which also is a part of the larger Gen Z protests. Speaking of this, there is also another country where students have protested and won politically but that's for the next section of this entry.

The protests developed into a nationwide mobilisation involving from high school students, parents, teachers and workers in general, to trade unions and many political organisations, including 2 political parties which are the French Communist Party and, more crucially, the La France Insoumise. Interesting enough, many organisations that involve in this are left-wing, including the 2 parties I mentioned above.

«Now, come on, protesting over bad school conditions is not worth it,» a submissive one may ask. Well, yes, if you consider classrooms being bloody overcrowded and hot (probably not related to some heatwaves), rats and cockroaches in school, deteriorating facilities, etc. as good school conditions. Additionally, high fuel prices (as mentioned above), inflation, goverment budget cut and many more things pretty much served as a fuel to this situation.

Now, just like several French riots occurred in the past, many frogs have been committing vandalism and looting, of course you have definitely heard about at least an incident of shops being robbed and basically destroyed. That said, I admire how the French can stand up for their rights at any time, not in a particularly safe way but still.

Serbia and its new student(?) government

On 25 October 2026, parliamentary elections were held in Serbia. Whilst elections were supposed to be due by December 2027, but Aleksandar Vučić — then president of the country — dissolved the National Assembly and called the elections on 9 September. Heh, many things happened in September that I didn't know.

Said elections were won by the Student List, an electoral list formed by... students, how surprising. Now you know what I meant by «students have won politically», how surprising, genuinely. But what about the «protested» part?

Now, Aleksandar Vučić has been an important figure in Serbia's politics for years, especially when he was the president of the country. Whilst being praised by supporters for economic growth and pragmatic diplomacy, this populist leader was also criticised for democratic backsling and corruption, the latter played an important role on what I'm going to tell.

The direct story began on 1 November 2024, when the concrete canopy of the main railway station in the city of Novi Sad collapsed onto the pavement below, killing 16 people and injuring another. Now, a tragic incident it was, but to many people, it was also the consequence of corruption within the government. Mass protests then began in the city, organising blockades of roads and heartquarters of Radio Television of Serbia and many more activities, such as a... protest cycling event? Heh, Serbian protests sound more fun than French protests.

Anyway, the protests then spread throughout the country. Students demanded «the publication of all documentation on the canopy's renovation, criminal accountability for those who attacked students of the Faculty of Dramatic Arts during a protest, the dropping of charges against students and demonstrators arrested at the 5 November 2024 protest in Novi Sad, and a 20% increase in university funding», etc. They then called for early elections in May 2025 citing the fact that the government did not meet their demands. But hold up, if they were demanding for elections, then who was going to run for them? Luckily, the students came up with the Student List, basically an electoral list which would include candidates the students preferred. That said, the electoral list was not founded until 9 September, the same day Vučić called for elections.

The Student List won 53.72% of the votes, which translates into 141 seats out of 250 in the National Assembly. Meanwhile, Vučić's list, the United Serbia, only got 39.47% of the votes, or 103 seats in Serbia's legislature, losing 26 seats. Most lists which already had at least a seat previously also experienced decreases in the number of seats.

Well, a students' victory in a place, aids another struggle of the students. While I don't take back my words here... well... interesting.
